Thurston Norm

Problem: Describe the ways in which $M^3$ fibers over $S^1$

Equivalently: Which $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$ are induced by a fibration $M \to S^1$?

Theorem (Thurston '86)
There exists a polyhedron $P \subset H^1(M;\,\mathbb{R})$ such that:
  • $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$ fibered $\;\Rightarrow\;$ $\phi$ primitive, lies in cone over open top-dimensional face of $P$
  • For each cone over an open face, all primitive classes are fibered or none are
  • And more!

Alexander Norm

A computational tool for the Thurston norm

Theorem (McMullen '98)
There exists a polyhedron $P_A$ such that:
  • $P_T \subset P_A$
  • Equivalently: $\|\cdot\|_A \leq \|\cdot\|_T$
  • $\|\phi\|_A = \|\phi\|_T$ on fibered classes
Theorem (Dunfield '99)
There are fibered 3-manifolds with $\|\cdot\|_A \neq \|\cdot\|_T$
Theorem (Ozsváth–Szabó '06)
For alternating link complements, $\|\cdot\|_A = \|\cdot\|_T$

Thurston Norm

Recall: $H^1(M;\,\mathbb{R}) \cong H_2(M,\,\partial M;\,\mathbb{R})$

Definition of Thurston Norm

"Minimal complexity of a dual surface"

  • For connected $S$, set $\;\chi_-(S) := \max\{0,\,-\chi(S)\}$
  • For $S = S_1 \sqcup \cdots \sqcup S_k$, set $\;\chi_-(S) = \displaystyle\sum_{i} \chi_-(S_i)$
  • Thurston norm: $\quad\|\phi\|_T \;:=\; \min\bigl\{\,\chi_-(S) \;\big|\; S \text{ dual to } \phi\,\bigr\}$

Alexander Norm

Definition of Alexander Norm
For most $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$, $$\|\phi\|_A \;:=\; \deg\bigl(\phi(\Delta_M)\bigr)$$
  • The Alexander polynomial $\Delta_M$ of $M$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}[H_1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})] = \mathbb{Z}[t_1^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_k^{\pm 1}]$
  • For complement of $2$-component link:
    • $H_1(M;\,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}^2$, generated by meridians $t_1, t_2$
    • Elements of multiplicative $\mathbb{Z}^2$: $\;1,\; t_1,\; t_2^{-1},\; t_1 t_2^{-4},\; t_1^3 t_2^2$
    • $\mathbb{Z}[H_1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})] = \mathbb{Z}[t_1^{\pm 1}, t_2^{\pm 1}]$
    • Variables of $\Delta_M$ $\longleftrightarrow$ meridional basis for $H_1$
  • Variables of $\Delta_M$ $\longleftrightarrow$ meridional basis for $H_1$
  • $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$ acts on $\Delta_M$
    • $(p,q) \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$ sends $t_1 \mapsto x^p$ and $t_2 \mapsto x^q$
    • $\phi\colon \langle t_1, t_2 \rangle \mapsto \langle x \rangle$, i.e. multiplicative $\mathbb{Z}^2 \to$ multiplicative $\mathbb{Z}$
Example: $\;\Delta_N = 1 - t_2 + t_2^2 + t_1 t_2 + t_1^{-1} t_2$

$\alpha = (1,0)$

$\beta = (1,2)$

$t_1 \mapsto x,\; t_2 \mapsto 1$

$t_1 \mapsto x,\; t_2 \mapsto x^2$

$\alpha(\Delta_N) = x^{-1} + 1 + x$

$\beta(\Delta_N) = x^4 + x^3 - x^2 + x + 1$

$\|\alpha\|_A = 2$

$\|\beta\|_A = 4$

The Main Theorem

Thurston Norm
$$\|\phi\|_T \;:=\; \min\bigl\{\,\chi_-(S) \;\big|\; S \text{ dual to } \phi\,\bigr\}$$
Alexander Norm
$$\|\phi\|_A \;:=\; \deg\bigl(\phi(\Delta_M)\bigr)$$
  • $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$ defines infinite cyclic cover $M_\phi$
  • Key Lemma 1: For most $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$, $\;\deg\bigl(\phi(\Delta_M)\bigr) + 1 = b_1(M_\phi)$
  • Key Lemma 2: For $S$ dual to $\phi$, $\;b_1(M_\phi) \leq b_1(S)$
Theorem (McMullen '98)
For any $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{R})$, $\;\|\phi\|_A \leq \|\phi\|_T$
Proof: For most $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$, $$\|\phi\|_T + 1 = b_1(S) \geq b_1(M_\phi) = \|\phi\|_A + 1.$$
The norm is continuous on $H^1(M;\,\mathbb{R})$. $\square$
We will focus on Key Lemma 1:
For most $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$, $\;\deg\bigl(\phi(\Delta_M)\bigr) + 1 = b_1(M_\phi)$

The Alexander Module

  • $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$ defines infinite cyclic cover $M_\phi$
  • $\pi_1(M, p) \to H_1(M)$ defines maximal abelian cover $\widetilde{M}$
Alexander Module $A_M$
The Alexander module of $M$ is $A_M = H_1(\widetilde{M},\, \widetilde{p};\,\mathbb{Z})$, viewed as a module over $R = \mathbb{Z}[H_1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})]$.
Alexander Module $A_\phi$
The Alexander module of $(M, \phi)$ is $A_\phi = H_1(M_\phi,\, \widetilde{p};\,\mathbb{Z})$, viewed as a module over $\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm 1}]$.

A Module Invariant

  • A presentation for an $R$-module $A$:
$R^m \xrightarrow{\;X\;} R^n \to A$$\displaystyle ,\qquad A = \frac{R^n}{X \cdot R^m}$
  • $X$ is a presentation matrix for $A$
Elementary Ideals
$E_i(A) \lhd R$ is the ideal generated by the $(n-i) \times (n-i)$ minors of $X$
Alexander Polynomial
$\Delta_A = \gcd\bigl(E_1(A)\bigr)$
Example:
  • $M = S^3 - L$
  • $A_M = H_1(\widetilde{M},\, \widetilde{p};\,\mathbb{Z})$
  • $\Delta_{A_M} =$ multivariable Alexander polynomial of $L$
  • $\phi = (1, \ldots, 1)$, $\;A_\phi = H_1(M_\phi,\, \widetilde{p};\,\mathbb{Z})$
  • $\Delta_{A_\phi} =$ Alexander polynomial of $L$
Write $\Delta_M := \Delta_{A_M}$, $\;\Delta_\phi := \Delta_{A_\phi}$
Lemma
$(x-1)\,\phi(\Delta_M) = \Delta_\phi$
Key Lemma 1: For most $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$, $\;\deg\bigl(\phi(\Delta_M)\bigr) + 1 = b_1(M_\phi)$

Elementary Ideals

$R^m \xrightarrow{\;X\;} R^n \to A$
Elementary Ideals
$E_i(A)$: ideal of $(n\!-\!i)\!\times\!(n\!-\!i)$ minors of $X$
Alexander Polynomial
$\Delta_A = \gcd\bigl(E_1(A)\bigr)$
  • When $R$ is a PID, Smith normal form: $$X \;\mapsto\; \left(\begin{array}{ccccccc} f_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & f_2 & \cdots & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & f_r & 0 & \cdots & 0 \end{array}\right), \qquad f_1 \mid f_2 \mid \cdots \mid f_r$$
  • Row/column operations do not alter $E_i(A)$: $$E_i(A) = (f_1 \cdots f_{n-i})$$
Structure Theorem
$A \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r} R/(f_i)$
invariant factors
Elementary ideals generalize invariant factors to non-PIDs

Degree of the Alexander Polynomial

  • $\phi \in H^1(M;\,\mathbb{Z})$, $\;A_\phi = H_1(M_\phi,\, \widetilde{p};\,\mathbb{Z})$ over $R = \mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm 1}]$
Theorem
If $\Delta_\phi \neq 0$, $\;\deg(\Delta_\phi) = b_1(M_\phi)$
Proof.
  • $R \oplus H_1(M_\phi;\,\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong_R\; H_1(M_\phi,\, \widetilde{p};\,\mathbb{Z})$
  • A presentation matrix for $A_\phi$:
    $$\left(\begin{array}{ccc} f_1 & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & f_r \\ \hline 0 & \cdots & 0 \end{array}\right)$$
  • $A_\phi \cong R \oplus R/(f_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(f_r), \qquad \Delta_\phi = f_1 \cdots f_r$
  • $\dim_\mathbb{Z}\bigl(R/(f_i)\bigr) = \deg(f_i)$, therefore...
$$b_1(M_\phi) = \sum_i \deg(f_i) = \deg(\Delta_\phi) \qquad \square$$
